N 03SecurityOnly relevant if you turn sync on

The server stores locked boxes. Only you hold the key.

You only need this page if you turn on encrypted sync. Without sync, your data never leaves your device.

600kPBKDF2 rounds per passphrase

§ 01The idea in one paragraph
01

Every record is locked on your device before it leaves.

When you turn on sync, your device locks every record in its own box before sending it, and only you have the key. The server stores locked boxes. It can count them and see when they changed, but it cannot open them. Your passphrase is what unlocks the key, and your passphrase never leaves your devices.

§ 03What the server seesTable 1
03

What the server can and cannot see.

The server can see

  • Your account email and sign-in times
  • Which tables exist and how many records each has
  • When records were created, changed or deleted
  • The size of each encrypted record
  • The wrapped master key and salts, which are useless without your passphrase or recovery code

The server cannot see

  • The text, amounts, dates or names inside any record
  • Your passphrase, recovery code or unwrapped keys
  • Which task, goal, transaction or journal entry a box is
  • Anything in device-only settings, which never sync

Sizes and counts are a real leak of metadata: a very long journal entry makes a bigger box. If that matters to you, keep sync off.

§ 04Who holds which key
04

Nobody can recover your passphrase. That is the point.

Passphrase and recovery code
They live in your head or your password manager. Northstar cannot recover them.
Row-level security
In your Supabase project, your signed-in account can only read and write its own rows. The app never contains a service-role key.
Conflicts
Resolved by the newest change, using a timestamp inside the ciphertext, so the server cannot reorder your edits.
Lose both and the cloud copy is gone
If you lose your passphrase and your recovery code, nobody can decrypt the cloud copy, including you, Supabase and Northstar. The readable copy on each device is unaffected, so export a backup.
§ 05Limits
05

What this does not protect against.

  1. Someone with your unlocked device or browser profile. The local database is readable by the app. Use your device’s screen lock and disk encryption.
  2. Malware on your device, or a weak passphrase. Choose a long one.
  3. Backup files you export. They are plain JSON. Store them carefully.

The exact network paths for every opt-in feature are on the Privacy page. Setup instructions for sync are in the repository under supabase/README.md.
